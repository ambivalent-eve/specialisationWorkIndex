Brief summary of the project!

This work will cover my advancement towards creating an audio instrument, where I will be creating my own dynamic soundtracker for Dungeons and Dragons.

Chosen Design Scenario!

The chosen design scenario was a dynamic soundtrack-er, where the idea was to create an adaptable soundtrack for different environments and activities. User control over dynamic soundtrackers were often minimal, usually involving the user selecting a playlist and letting the music go in the background.

Final Project!

In the end, I crafted a dynamic soundtracker for D&D environments! The idea was to create a sort of audio player that dungeon masters and players would use for their games. Playing music to set the mood, putting on ambiences for the atmosphere or even playing sound effects for added in immersion. Thus, I created a soundtracker that basically responds to that, with multiple songs with different genres to choose from as well as ambience sounds one could toggle on to create a unique atmosphere!

Features and Design Philosophies!

As what was primarily an audio player, I was ensuring that the user would be met with conventional audio controls so that they wouldn't have to learn any new models. This was the simplest interaction in my project. A simple play and pause function, a skip and rewind function as well as a shuffle and loop function. To make these functions work, I've used addEventListener various times within the code, most primarily click as users would have to click on various buttons to activate certain functions.

One important extended technique I've put great emphasis on however was the Math.random technique. As a soundtracker, audiences will most likely want a shuffled playlist, so that no one experience is ever the same. The way the code works is that it shuffles the rest of the queue that's already established.

One method I had for adding increased customisability was the feature to add songs and playlists on the left to queues through the click of the button. DMs who were selective with their music can now add their tracks one by one, or select the entire playlist to queue. I've incorporated such a feature because I thought that there would be people who'd want to spend as little input as possible. To help with that, I've added playlists that indicate what sort of mood the songs will bring and brought a minimum of three songs each playlist. Additionally, the first playlist the user lands on is the 'all songs' playlist for those who'd rather hop in a D&D session and play all the songs straight away.

In accordance with the design philosophy of taking into account that there are people who'd minimise their inputs, I've incoporated a proper loop function that doesn't loop the one singular song but rather the whole queue. This means that if the user decides to add all the songs in queue and loop them, they'd have an endless stream of music that would always start over every time the queue ends.

Another feature I've incoporated for added dynamic immersion was the addition of ambience sounds. These sounds could be played alongside any soundtrack, and the user can select multiple as well! As dynamic soundtrackers want to account for different environments and atmospheres, I wanted to create the ability to manipulate the atmospheres through sound.

This is done through associating each of them through a button ID that upon "click" would play the ambience in the audio player. Furthermore, users could go even further and customise the pitch of the song themselves, which could result in slowed-down, melachonic tracks or fast paced, energetic tracks. This is done through sliders, a quick and intuitive action. With these sliders, users can also change the volume of the songs or change the timestamps of the song to arrive at different points.

To expand further on my desire to make the website feel interactive, I wanted to make it so that hovering your mouse over interactives or clicking on them actually gave responses. Thus, I made it so that every time the user hovered over the buttons, it became a teal like colour to signify that it was interactive. Clicking on said button would change its size a little as well, but another thing I've added was making it so that pressing the tab button on the keyboard could actually present which buttons were interactable and give you options to play certain buttons through only your keyboard.

An accessibility feature I've added was light mode and dark mode preferences. If the user preferred light mode on their browser, then they would receive the light mode colour scheme. I incoporated this because as a dynamic soundtracker, there may be times where you could be outside where the lens flare makes it difficult to see your screen. Thus, I wanted to add the toggles between the two in order for the user to adapt to more scenarios for tabletop gaming, as not all D&D takes place inside, sometimes players play outside as well in the sun.

Finally, one feature I've added was a small soundboard. This allows players and DMs to incoporate small, easy sounds for their world, primarily combat. DMs could play the sound of magic as they unleash lightning bolts, or maybe players could play the sound of swords swinging against the air to represent themselves. Though this does not adhere to being a dynamic soundtracker, one of the components of the brief was adapting to any solo or group dynamics. As D&D is a group activity that involves a lot of combat, it only makes sense to also include sound effects to throw inside the audio player for increased immersion.
